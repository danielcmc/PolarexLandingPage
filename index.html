01 The prospecting gap

Account fit doesn’t
explain the timing.

A company can fit your market without having a reason to evaluate your product. Even a promising change needs context.

Hiring for OpenTelemetry could mean a new platform evaluation, an in-house build, or expansion of an existing vendor. Each calls for a different sales approach.

Your team needs to know what changed, whether the decision is still open, and what makes your product relevant.

Enterprise observability modernization

Seller perspective: Grafana Cloud (hypothetical)

Recommended action

Observed in public sources

  • A 9 August leadership job posting describes an EIT roadmap to move from fragmented monitoring tools to an OpenTelemetry-native approach across hybrid and cloud infrastructure. [1]
  • The same role assigns responsibility for telemetry volumes, licensing, and ingestion costs. [1]
  • The FY2026 10-K independently supports the wider cloud-modernization strategy. It does not establish an observability vendor evaluation. [2]

Source boundary: the observability-specific findings come from one job posting. Multiple claims from that posting are not independent buying signals.

Polarex interpretation

A managed observability or telemetry-cost discussion could be relevant to Grafana Cloud’s Adaptive Telemetry capabilities [5] (opens in a new tab). Product fit is plausible; an active vendor evaluation remains unverified.

Alternative explanations to investigate
  1. A new managed platform. Consolidation may create an opportunity if the vendor decision is still open.
  2. Instrumentation standardization. OpenTelemetry may be adopted while existing commercial backends stay in place.
  3. An internal or cloud-provider approach. Self-hosted open source or Google Cloud tooling could weaken Grafana Cloud’s fit.
